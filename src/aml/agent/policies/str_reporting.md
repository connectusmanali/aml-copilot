# Suspicious transaction reports

## When to report
In Canada, a reporting entity files a suspicious transaction report (STR) with FINTRAC when there are reasonable grounds to suspect that a transaction or attempted transaction is related to money laundering or terrorist financing. The report is due as soon as practicable once that conclusion is reached. This note summarises guidance for this project and is not legal advice.

## What the narrative must contain
Who is involved, what happened, when and where it happened, and why it is suspicious. Every statement must be supported by the bank's records: transaction identifiers, dates and amounts. State facts, not speculation, and explain the indicators that led to the suspicion.

## Decision rules for this system
The agent never files or closes a case. A human investigator approves, edits or rejects the draft and records a reason. Watchlist matches and cases the agent could not complete within its budget always go to a person.
